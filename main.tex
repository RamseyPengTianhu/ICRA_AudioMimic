\begin{abstract}
Streaming dance must prepare for upcoming music from audio heard so far while
continuing movements that have already been committed and can no longer be
revised. We present \method{}, a framework that generates robot-native dance
from music as it arrives. A frozen music foundation model forecasts future
musical context using heard audio alone. A kinematics-guided structure--detail
representation gives whole-body organization and local articulation
complementary roles, while \cof{} trains continuation from jointly generated
motion and its resulting reference state. Together, these components couple
musical anticipation with successive motion decisions. On 60-second FineDance
generations, a frozen music--motion evaluator favors the intended soundtrack
over alternative tracks in approximately 75\% of pairwise comparisons.
Separate matched training comparisons identify a tradeoff between motion
distribution fidelity and smoothness. These findings support soundtrack
correspondence in continuously generated references within a framework that
connects musical foresight to committed movement.
\end{abstract}
