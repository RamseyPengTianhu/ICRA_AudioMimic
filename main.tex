\title{Duet: Discrete–Continuous Motion Generation for Music-Driven Humanoid Dance}

% \author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Tianhu Peng et al.}
% \IEEEauthorblockA{UCL Robotics Institute, University College London}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Humanoid dance should be coherent and expressive, appropriate to the music being heard, and realizable by a physical controller. We present \method{}, a robot-native causal dance generation and execution system. Its kinematics-guided discrete--continuous (D+C) representation assigns complementary coarse-structure and fine-detail roles through explicit supervision and jointly decodes both into native robot motion. \cof{} constructs sampled reference transitions and trains continuation from their committed history and reconstructed boundary states. A frozen causal music model supplies timestamped forecasts from arrived audio, and a reference bridge connects committed motion to a fixed SONIC whole-body tracker. Evaluation separates music--motion matching, explicit rhythmic and dynamic adaptation, and dance quality at generation and execution layers. Formal generator comparisons and controlled replay characterize musical matching and downstream tracking limitations. In a two-training-seed codec study, adding the continuous branch reduces structure/detail reconstruction error by approximately 30\%/45\% relative to discrete-only decoding. The proposed supervision raises the discrete-to-continuous structural intervention ratio from 1.20 to 1.41, while continuous-code effective rank reaches 15.1 of 16, supporting a measurable complementary representation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ================================================================
Humanoid robots are increasingly able to translate external
information into coordinated whole-body behavior.
Language-conditioned motion generation enables robots to perform
actions described in natural language~\cite{jiang2025harmon},
while vision-based control allows them to adapt their movements
to terrain and obstacles~\cite{zhuang2025humanoid}.
These advances motivate richer forms of embodied interaction,
including movement guided by music. Music-responsive dance
requires a robot to transform an evolving auditory signal into
coherent physical expression, with movements that reflect the
soundtrack as it unfolds.

Music provides a distinctive conditioning signal. It typically
does not prescribe a unique action sequence; instead, rhythm,
accents, and dynamic variation guide when and how movements
unfold. Multiple dances can suit the same soundtrack, but
appropriate responses must combine musical correspondence with
coordinated and varied motion. For a physical humanoid, this
relationship must also accommodate the robot's body and remain
perceptible after execution. The objective is therefore to
generate dance whose musical suitability is expressed through
the robot's actual movement.

Music-to-dance models have made substantial progress in
choreographic quality, genre modeling, and whole-body
coordination~\cite{tseng2023edge,li2023finedance,
yang2025megadance,li2025soulnet}.
Recent work has further established important foundations for
robotic performance. DiscoForcing generates motion causally
from streaming audio and transfers human-format motion to a
humanoid through retargeting and tracking~\cite{ji2026discoforcing}.
RoboPerform integrates audio-conditioned expression into a
teacher--student action policy for music-driven dance and
speech-accompanying gestures~\cite{li2026roboperform}.
These approaches demonstrate complementary routes from sound
to embodied behavior.

An open methodological question is how to learn a robot-native
dance reference generator that preserves both whole-body
organization and music-dependent local variation throughout
recursive generation. Generating in a human motion space leaves
the mapping of these properties to the robot to a downstream
retargeting stage. Integrating expression directly into an
action policy couples its realization to the learned controller.
We investigate a complementary design in which musical motion
is planned directly in the robot's native space and delivered
to an independently trained whole-body controller.
This requires more than removing an online conversion step:
the representation must accommodate structural coordination
and expressive detail, and the continuation model must preserve
their consistency as its own outputs become future inputs.
Whether these properties survive tracking is a further
question that must be evaluated at the execution level.

We address the representation problem with a kinematics-guided
discrete--continuous (D+C) design learned from dance data
retargeted to the robot. A discrete vocabulary captures reusable
motion patterns, while a continuous residual supplies variation
beyond quantization. Our supervision explicitly encourages
complementary roles: designated coarse kinematic structure is
supervised through the discrete branch, while the combined
representation reconstructs the full motion. Structural
supervision under residual corruption further encourages this
organization to remain robust to changes in the continuous
component. During generation, both branches receive music
conditions; a discrete planner predicts structure, and a
conditional diffusion model generates the residual given that
structure and the available reference context. Their combination
is decoded into native robot motion by a shared
state-conditioned decoder.

Preserving this organization over time requires training on
the context created by motion commitment. Teacher-forced
histories differ from the model-generated histories encountered
during recursive inference, a challenge addressed broadly by
generated-history and diffusion-forcing
methods~\cite{bengio2015scheduled,chen2024diffusionforcing}.
For our generator, however, a reference transition comprises
discrete codes, continuous residuals, and the boundary state
derived from their decoded motion. We introduce \cof{} to
construct these components jointly during training.
A first pass samples and commits a complete D+C reference
prefix, updating both the latent history and the reference
boundary state. A second pass learns continuation from this
generated context. This procedure exposes the continuation
model to the coupled consequences of its own structural and
detail predictions.

We integrate these components into a causal music-to-robot
system, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.
A frozen music forecaster provides predicted conditioning
features from observed audio without accessing future
waveforms. The generator continuously plans and commits
robot-native references, which are delivered to the fixed
SONIC whole-body controller~\cite{luo2026sonic}.
The generator maintains its committed reference context,
while SONIC uses measured robot state for physical feedback
control. Our evaluation connects the proposed mechanisms
to three outcomes: motion quality, musical suitability,
and their retention during execution. Representation
interventions and matched continuation-training comparisons
examine the roles of D+C and \cof{}, while controlled music
pairings and reference--execution comparisons assess whether
the resulting dance responds appropriately to its soundtrack.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Kinematics-guided robot-native D+C generation:}
    a motion representation and music-conditioned generator
    that establish complementary structural and detail roles
    through explicit kinematic supervision.

    \item \textbf{Commit Forcing for consistent continuation:}
    a training procedure that jointly commits generated
    structure and detail and reconstructs the corresponding
    reference boundary state, enabling continuation learning
    from complete model-generated reference transitions.

    \item \textbf{Causal musical performance with paired
    generation--execution evaluation:}
    a streaming system connecting predicted music conditions
    and robot-native planning to fixed whole-body control,
    together with an evaluation protocol that examines
    musical suitability and motion quality at both layers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Learning Whole-Body Control and Generative Planning}

Reinforcement learning provides an effective framework for
learning humanoid control, while motion imitation supplies
demonstrations of coordinated behaviors that are difficult to
specify through task rewards alone.
PHC develops reusable motion tracking for simulated
humanoids~\cite{luo2023phc}, and ExBody2, BeyondMimic, and
SONIC advance expressive, agile, and scalable control on
humanoid robots~\cite{ji2024exbody2,liao2025beyondmimic,
luo2026sonic}.
These approaches establish the physical execution capabilities
on which higher-level motion generation can build.

Generative models extend this repertoire beyond replaying
individual reference clips.
BeyondMimic combines motion tracking with latent state--action
diffusion, using guidance to compose behaviors and satisfy
downstream objectives~\cite{liao2025beyondmimic}.
An alternative is to retain an explicit motion reference
between planning and control.
Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026wholebody} generate terrain-aware
robot references with diffusion, track them using RL, and
fine-tune the tracker with a frozen generator in a closed-loop
setting.
PARC uses physics-based tracking to correct generated motions
and iteratively expand the training dataset for terrain
traversal~\cite{xu2025parc}.
These works demonstrate complementary ways to connect
generative motion models with physical control.
Our work adopts reference-based execution with a fixed
SONIC controller and focuses on music-conditioned robot-native
generation. Commit Forcing updates generated reference
context during training; it does not use physically executed
trajectories to refine the generator or tracker.

\subsection{Motion Representations and Reusable Motor Priors}

Learned motion representations support both generative
modeling and reusable physical skills.
Neural Categorical Priors learn a discrete skill space through
RL-based imitation and use prior shifting to improve
behavioral coverage for downstream control~\cite{zhu2023ncp}.
GPC jointly learns an FSQ-based motion vocabulary and a
control policy through RL, then models skill sequences with
an autoregressive transformer~\cite{shi2026gpc}.
These representations provide latent interfaces to
physics-based control policies.
Our latent representation instead decodes into an explicit
robot motion reference that is executed by a separate tracker.

For kinematic motion generation, T2M-GPT and MoMask use
discrete motion tokens~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt,guo2024momask},
whereas MLD performs diffusion in a continuous latent
space~\cite{chen2023mld}.
Hybrid approaches connect these modeling choices in different
ways. DisCoRD reconstructs continuous motion from discrete
tokens through rectified-flow decoding~\cite{cho2025discord}.
Most closely, DC-Motion represents human motion using
discrete structural tokens and continuous residual latents,
with masked structural prediction followed by conditional
residual diffusion~\cite{wang2026dcmotion}.
Our work shares the motivation for complementary discrete
and continuous components, while studying their roles in
robot-native streaming generation.
We introduce designated robot-kinematic supervision for the
discrete and residual-corrupted reconstructions, and condition
decoding on the reference boundary state.
The resulting components are generated and committed
jointly, allowing their structural and detail roles to be
examined through controlled interventions.

\subsection{Music Representations, Dance Generation, and Robotic Performance}

Music conditioning ranges from explicit signal descriptors
to learned audio representations.
Features such as onset strength, tempo, and chroma,
available through librosa~\cite{mcfee2015librosa}, provide
interpretable rhythmic and spectral information.
Pretrained representations offer complementary information:
EDGE uses embeddings from the generative music model
Jukebox~\cite{tseng2023edge,dhariwal2020jukebox}, while
Music2Vec, MERT, MusicFM, and MuQ learn transferable
music representations through self-supervised
training~\cite{li2022music2vec,li2023mert,
won2023musicfm,zhu2025muq}.
CLAP, MuLan, and MuQ-MuLan align audio with natural
language, supporting semantic retrieval and
conditioning~\cite{wu2022clap,huang2022mulan,zhu2025muq}.
These approaches provide different sources of acoustic
and semantic information; their suitability for streaming
dance additionally depends on temporal resolution,
accessible audio context, and inference latency.

Music-conditioned dance generation builds on these
representations to model movement and musical correspondence.
FACT and Bailando develop sequence generation and discrete
choreographic modeling~\cite{li2021aist,siyao2022bailando},
while EDGE introduces diffusion-based editable
choreography~\cite{tseng2023edge}.
FineDance expands fine-grained full-body dance
data~\cite{li2023finedance}; LODGE, MEGADance, and SoulNet
address long-form generation, genre-aware modeling,
and coordinated holistic motion,
respectively~\cite{li2024lodge,yang2025megadance,
li2025soulnet}.
These advances establish foundations for expressive dance
generation and music--motion matching.

Generative audio models also offer a route to predictive
conditioning.
MusicGen models compressed audio-token sequences for
controllable music synthesis~\cite{copet2023musicgen},
while Live Music Models and Magenta RealTime~2 support
streaming music generation~\cite{gdmlyria2025live,
magenta2026mrt2}.
In our system, a frozen MRT2 model forecasts music states
from arrived audio, which condition subsequent motion
generation.
This use of predicted states makes audio visibility and
the remaining forecast lead explicit parts of the
music-to-motion interface.

Conditioned motion generation has further reached
physical humanoids.
HARMON generates robot whole-body motion from language
descriptions~\cite{jiang2025harmon}.
RoboPerform uses audio-conditioned style within a
teacher--student control framework for dance and
speech-accompanying gestures~\cite{li2026roboperform}.
DiscoForcing connects causal streaming audio to
human-format motion generation and humanoid execution
through retargeting and tracking~\cite{ji2026discoforcing}.
Our approach performs retargeting during data preparation
and learns a robot-native D+C reference generator, with
music conditioning both discrete planning and continuous
residual generation.
We study musical suitability and motion quality through
paired generation--execution evaluation with a fixed
whole-body controller.

\subsection{Streaming Generation and Training with Generated Context}

Recursive generation introduces a mismatch between
dataset-provided training histories and model-generated
inference histories.
Scheduled Sampling progressively substitutes model predictions
for observed sequence inputs~\cite{bengio2015scheduled}.
Professor Forcing uses adversarial training to align recurrent
dynamics between teacher-forced and free-running
modes~\cite{lamb2016professor}.
Diffusion Forcing trains sequence models with independent
per-token noise levels, supporting causal generation and
flexible conditioning on partially noised
histories~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing}.
In motion synthesis, MotionStreamer combines causal motion
compression with autoregressive diffusion and a Two-Forward
strategy that replaces a subset of ground-truth latents with
first-pass predictions~\cite{xiao2025motionstreamer}.
DiscoForcing further develops temporal scheduling and
history guidance to balance continuity with responsiveness
to changing audio~\cite{ji2026discoforcing}.

Commit Forcing addresses a specific reference-construction
problem within this broader setting.
A committed D+C transition contains discrete structure,
continuous detail, and a boundary state reconstructed from
their jointly decoded motion.
We sample the structural and residual components with the
deployment samplers, commit their shared prefix, and train
the next continuation using the updated reference context.
This explicitly links history replacement to motion decoding
and boundary-state reconstruction.
The feedback remains within the reference-generation process;
measured physical state is used by the downstream controller.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Overview and Problem Formulation}

Our framework generates robot-native dance references
from causally available music and executes them through
a fixed whole-body controller.
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes representation
learning and online generation and execution.
Training proceeds in two stages: we first learn a
kinematics-guided discrete--continuous (D+C) motion
codec from retargeted human dance, and then train a
music-conditioned generator with Commit Forcing to
learn continuation from generated reference contexts.

At planning cycle $k$, the generator receives committed
latent history $\mathcal{H}_k$, reference boundary state
$b_k$, and predicted music condition $m_k$.
Let $P_k=\{(q_{0,k,i},r_{k,i})\}_{i=1}^{H}$ denote
an $H$-step latent plan, where $q_0$ is a discrete
code index and $r$ is a continuous residual.
We sample
\begin{equation}
    P_k \sim
    p_\theta(\cdot\mid\mathcal{H}_k,b_k,m_k).
    \label{eq:planning_objective}
\end{equation}
Only the first $C<H$ steps are committed.
Their decoded motion and latent codes update the
reference context before replanning.
The boundary state is derived from committed references;
physical state feedback is handled by the downstream
controller.

\subsection{Kinematics-Guided Discrete--Continuous
Motion Representation}
\label{sec:dc_representation}

\paragraph{Robot-native motion.}
We use paired music and human dance from
FineDance~\cite{li2023finedance}, with offline motion
retargeting to obtain Unitree G1 trajectories.
Each motion frame is represented by
\begin{equation}
    x_n =
    [\Delta p^{\mathrm{local}}_{xy},\,h,\,
    \sin\Delta\psi,\,\cos\Delta\psi,\,
    \boldsymbol{\vartheta}],
    \label{eq:native_motion}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta p^{\mathrm{local}}_{xy}$ is heading-local
planar displacement, $h$ is root height, $\Delta\psi$
is yaw change, and
$\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{R}^{29}$ contains
joint angles.
This 34-dimensional representation describes motion
relative to the evolving root frame.
Global planar position and heading are recovered
through deterministic $\mathrm{SE}(2)$ accumulation.

To condition continuation on the preceding reference,
we define the boundary state
\begin{equation}
    b =
    [h,\,v^{\mathrm{local}}_{xy},\,v_z,\,
    \dot{\psi},\,\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\,
    \dot{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}].
    \label{eq:boundary_state}
\end{equation}
It contains pose and first-order motion information
and conditions both encoding and decoding.

\paragraph{Discrete--continuous decomposition.}
A state-conditioned encoder maps a motion segment
to a latent sequence $z=E_\phi(x,b)$ with a temporal
downsampling factor of two.
Each latent vector is decomposed using a learned
codebook:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    q_{0,i}
    &=\arg\min_j\|z_i-e(j)\|_2^2,\\
    r_i
    &=z_i-\operatorname{sg}(e(q_{0,i})),
\end{split}
\label{eq:dc_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $e(j)$ is a codebook embedding and
$\operatorname{sg}$ denotes stop-gradient.
The discrete embeddings and residuals are combined
and decoded jointly:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}=D_\psi(e(q_0)+r,b).
    \label{eq:shared_decoding}
\end{equation}
The resulting representation couples a finite motion
vocabulary with continuous reconstruction capacity.

\paragraph{Kinematics-guided supervision.}
Full-motion reconstruction alone does not assign
explicit kinematic responsibilities to the two
components.
We therefore supervise discrete reconstruction on
a designated structural view while retaining
full-motion supervision for their joint reconstruction.

The full view $\Phi_f$ contains native motion,
boundary-aware velocities, current-root-relative
link and sole positions, and their velocities.
The structural view $\Phi_s$ selects root, leg,
and waist channels together with torso, wrist,
and sole geometry.
It specifies which aspects of whole-body organization
the discrete component is encouraged to preserve.

Let $e=e(q_0)$ and $r=z-\operatorname{sg}(e)$.
We construct three training paths:
\begin{align}
    \hat{x}_{f}
    &=D_\psi(\operatorname{sg}(e)+r,b),
    \label{eq:full_path}\\
    \hat{x}_{s}
    &=D_\psi(z+\operatorname{sg}(e-z),b),
    \label{eq:structure_path}\\
    \hat{x}_{r}
    &=D_\psi(\operatorname{sg}(e)+\mathcal{C}(r),b),
    \label{eq:robust_path}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{C}$ removes the residual or adds
small channel-scaled noise.
The discrete-only path uses a straight-through
estimator, while the corrupted-residual path
encourages structural reconstruction to remain
robust to residual variation.

For view $v\in\{f,s\}$, define
\begin{equation}
    d_v(\hat{x},x)
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_v|}
    \sum_{a\in\mathcal{B}_v}
    \operatorname{MSE}
    \bigl(
    \bar{\Phi}_{v,a}(\hat{x}),
    \bar{\Phi}_{v,a}(x)
    \bigr),
    \label{eq:kinematic_distance}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_v$ indexes the native-motion,
velocity, kinematic-position, and kinematic-velocity
blocks of that view.
The bar denotes normalization using training-set
statistics.
Averaging within each block before combining them
prevents high-dimensional geometry from dominating
the objective.

The codec loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{codec}}
    ={}&d_f(\hat{x}_f,x)
    +\lambda_s d_s(\hat{x}_s,x)\\
    &+\lambda_r d_s(\hat{x}_r,x)
    +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VQ}},
\end{split}
\label{eq:codec_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VQ}}$ comprises codebook
and commitment losses~\cite{vandenOord2017vqvae}.
This objective encourages discrete codes to preserve
designated structure and residuals to provide
complementary detail, without imposing independent
decoding of the two components.

\subsection{Causal Music-Conditioned Motion Generation}
\label{sec:music_generation}

\paragraph{Predicted music states.}
Arrived audio is tokenized with
SpectroStream~\cite{li2025spectrostream} and supplied
to a frozen Magenta RealTime~2
model~\cite{magenta2026mrt2}.
We roll the observed prefix forward and retain
14 predicted temporal music states, denoted FMS14.
These conditions are computed without accessing
future waveform samples.

\paragraph{Timing-aware conditioning.}
The forecaster and generator operate asynchronously.
Each predicted state retains its native timestamp,
so the nominal prediction horizon can be distinguished
from the lead remaining when a reference is due.
For forecast-state timestamp $\tau_j$ and reference
delivery deadline $d_k$, expressed on the same
audio-time axis, the effective lead is
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{k,j}=\tau_j-d_k.
    \label{eq:effective_lead}
\end{equation}
The generator selects the newest completed forecast
and masks states whose effective lead has expired.
The resulting music condition $m_k$ is supplied
to both motion branches.

We use FrameFiLM to introduce this condition through
feature-wise modulation~\cite{perez2018film}.
For an intermediate motion feature $u$, the
modulation has the form
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{FiLM}(u;m_k)
    =
    \gamma(m_k)\odot u+\beta(m_k),
    \label{eq:music_film}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are learned conditioning
mappings.
Music thus influences both discrete planning and
continuous residual generation.

\paragraph{Discrete planning and residual diffusion.}
Let $c_k=(\mathcal{H}_k,b_k,m_k)$ denote the shared
generation context.
We factorize the plan distribution as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    p_\theta(q_{0,1:H},r_{1:H}\mid c_k)
    ={}&
    \prod_{i=1}^{H}
    p_{\theta_D}(q_{0,i}\mid q_{0,<i},c_k)\\
    &\cdot
    p_{\theta_C}(r_{1:H}\mid q_{0,1:H},c_k).
\end{split}
\label{eq:generator_factorization}
\end{equation}
A causal Transformer samples the discrete sequence
autoregressively.
A conditional diffusion model then generates the
residual sequence using the sampled structure
and the shared context.
The discrete branch uses cross-entropy supervision.
The residual branch predicts clean residuals from
noised inputs and uses DDIM
sampling~\cite{song2021ddim} at inference.
The sampled embeddings and residuals are combined
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:shared_decoding} to produce
one robot-native reference plan.

\subsection{Commit Forcing for Streaming Generation}
\label{sec:commit_forcing}

\paragraph{Generated reference transitions.}
Streaming inference continues from previously
sampled structure, residuals, and their decoded
boundary state.
Teacher-forced training instead provides dataset
reference contexts.
Commit Forcing exposes the generator to a complete
generated reference transition before supervising
its continuation.

\paragraph{First pass: sample and commit.}
A stop-gradient first pass samples an $H$-step
D+C plan with the deployment sampling procedures.
Its first $C$ discrete codes and corresponding
residuals form one committed prefix.
The reference context is updated as
\begin{equation}
    (\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1},\tilde b_{k+1})
    =
    \mathcal{U}
    (\mathcal{H}_k,b_k,\tilde P_k^{1:C}),
    \label{eq:commit_update}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{U}$ appends the committed latent
prefix, retains the prescribed history length,
and reconstructs the next boundary state from
the decoded reference.
The unused plan suffix is discarded.

A training curriculum selects either a complete
generated transition or a complete dataset
transition, with the probability of selecting
the generated transition increasing to one.
Discrete codes, residuals, and their corresponding
boundary state are selected together.

\paragraph{Second pass: learn continuation.}
The second pass uses the updated reference context
and the music condition for the next horizon.
The discrete branch is supervised against the
next dataset code sequence.
A structural sequence is also sampled
autoregressively from the updated context to
condition residual training.

The dataset residual is defined relative to its
dataset code embedding.
When conditioning on a sampled code, we therefore
rebase the residual target.
Let $z_i^*=e(q_{0,i}^*)+r_i^*$ be the next dataset
target latent and $\tilde q_{0,i}$ the sampled
continuation code.
The rebased target is
\begin{equation}
    \tilde r_i^*=z_i^*-e(\tilde q_{0,i}),
    \label{eq:residual_rebasing}
\end{equation}
so that the sampled embedding and its residual
target sum to the complete target latent.
After residual normalization, the continuation
objective combines classification and denoising:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}
    =
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
    +\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}}.
    \label{eq:generator_objective}
\end{equation}

Each training window contains one sampled reference
transition followed by a supervised continuation.
The continuation retains the dataset target latent
under the updated boundary state; no
boundary-dependent re-encoding is performed.
Consequently, rebasing preserves the target latent,
while continuity across generated boundaries is
learned through state conditioning and exposure
to generated reference contexts.

\subsection{Robot Reference Integration and Execution}
\label{sec:robot_execution}

The generator operates in robot-native reference
space, and a dedicated bridge converts committed
motion into inputs for the pretrained SONIC
whole-body controller~\cite{luo2026sonic}.
The bridge accumulates heading-local root
displacements and yaw changes, maps joint references
to the controller convention, and resamples the
30-Hz motion onto a continuous 50-Hz reference grid.
Only committed motion is delivered; the uncommitted
plan suffix is excluded.

SONIC remains fixed and combines the consumed
reference with measured robot state to produce
control actions.
The generator separately updates its latent
history and boundary state from committed reference
motion.
Thus, reference continuation and physical tracking
use distinct feedback paths: generated references
condition subsequent planning, while measured
execution closes the controller loop.
The reference delivery and controller configuration
are held fixed in paired execution comparisons.

\section{Discussion}
% ================================================================
\textbf{What is supported.} The representation ablation supports complementary
structure/detail roles. The matched replay demonstrates sensitivity to
consumption age even when condition content is held fixed. Tracking the same
recorded trajectories reveals another loss of motion dynamics. Improving
condition timing therefore does not, by itself, establish preserved physical
expression; conversely, ideal-age cached conditions do not eliminate tracking
attenuation.

\textbf{What remains unresolved.} The age-attribution study uses one song and one
sampling seed with an artificial empty-history start. Its trajectory RMSE is
not an aesthetic score. Physical tracking results are MuJoCo measurements,
not a real-hardware validation or safety guarantee. The learned standing-start
candidate has not passed its generation/execution gates. FineDance formal
comparisons, smaller mechanism pilots, and the two-training-seed codec study
have different cohorts and cannot be pooled. Additional architecture and
Commit-Forcing ablations, a calibrated cross-dataset evaluator, and human
judgments are still needed.

\textbf{Research direction.} We prioritize startup train--deployment consistency
and deadline-effective music conditioning before adding model branches. If
tracking-aware reference adaptation is pursued, it should preserve validated
musical events under a fixed controller and be compared with simple smoothing
and clipping. The controller remains fixed in the present study. The final held-out set must be
separate from songs repeatedly used to tune the present diagnostics.

% ================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ================================================================
We presented \method{} toward coherent, expressive robot dance that responds
appropriately to music as it arrives. Kinematics-guided D+C modeling assigns
complementary coarse-structure and fine-detail roles in native robot motion;
\cof{} trains continuation from jointly committed generated references and
their boundary states. Timestamped causal forecasts and a reference bridge
connect this generator to fixed SONIC execution. The evaluation separates MMR,
explicit music adaptation, and dance quality/aesthetics, with runtime and
tracking diagnostics identifying where intended behavior is lost. Existing
representation and layered studies support specific mechanisms, not a claim
that online executed dance already matches offline animation. Matched learning
ablations, learned-startup validation, and music-fit and aesthetic judgments at
both generation and execution remain necessary for that broader conclusion.
