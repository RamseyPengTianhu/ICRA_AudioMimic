\title{AudioMimic: Factorized Motion Coding, Commit Forcing, and Anticipatory Music Conditioning for Streaming Humanoid Dance}

% \author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Tianhu Peng et al.}
% \IEEEauthorblockA{UCL Robotics Institute, University College London}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Online humanoid dance is not offline choreography played one window at a time: a streaming system must preserve motion structure and detail, continue from its own committed motion, and prepare for musical development without observing future audio. \method{} addresses these requirements as one causal planning chain. A factorized robot-motion codec represents each plan with a discrete structural token and a continuous detail residual; \cof{} trains continuation from generated motion history and its reconstructed robot state; and a causal music forecaster supplies observed and predicted-future conditions from arrived audio alone. Matched interventions across two independent training seeds show that structural-token changes have approximately 1.41 times the declared structural effect of residual changes, while the residual preserves complementary full-body variation. The integrated motion foundation completes 156 strict-causal one-minute rollouts with finite, upright motion and without a boundary-specific discontinuity. Together, these results establish a stable robot-native basis for streaming dance and a causally valid route to anticipatory music conditioning.
\end{abstract}

% ================================================================
\section{Introduction}
% ================================================================
Music-to-dance systems are commonly designed as offline sequence generators: the complete song is available, a complete motion is synthesized, and physical execution is handled afterward. A live humanoid changes the problem. Future audio has not arrived, each plan must finish before the next action deadline, and every executed segment changes the state from which the next segment begins. A streaming dancer must therefore solve three coupled questions: how to represent a plan without losing either structure or detail, how to learn from its own evolving motion history, and how to prepare for music that has not yet been heard.

\method{} follows the same three-part logic. Its factorized motion codec supplies a discrete structural vocabulary together with a continuous detail channel. \cof{} replaces purely teacher-provided history with the generated context produced by the streaming loop. The causal music interface then derives two complementary conditions from arrived audio: one summarizes what has been heard, and the other predicts what is likely to follow. Together, these modules turn an offline sequence problem into a repeating causal loop of listening, anticipating, planning, executing, and replanning.

The codec is the foundation of this loop. A single continuous stream can preserve local detail but provides no explicit structural variable for support transitions and large-scale pose changes. A discrete tokenizer provides a structural alphabet but can discard articulation and within-structure variation. We combine them and make their roles testable: the token is supervised through a robot-specific structural view, the residual restores complementary full-body detail, and their combination reconstructs native G1 motion from the current robot state.

The next challenge is continuation. At every cycle, the system predicts 0.53~s of motion, executes 0.27~s, reconstructs the resulting robot state, and replans. \cof{} trains across this same generated-history transition, so the model learns to recover from the context created by its earlier plans rather than seeing clean ground-truth history throughout training. The third challenge is anticipation. Since true future audio is unavailable, \method{} rolls a causal music model forward from the observed prefix and conditions motion on the resulting predicted-future state. This is a forecast, not hidden waveform lookahead. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the complete story.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a factorized robot-motion codec that gives the planner a discrete structural vocabulary and a continuous detail channel, with projector supervision and intervention tests that verify their distinct roles;
    \item \cof{}, a generated-history training procedure that learns each new plan from the motion history and reconstructed robot state produced by previous model outputs;
    \item an anticipatory causal music interface that derives observed and predicted-future music states from arrived audio and assigns them to the structural and detail branches.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% ================================================================
\subsection{Music-Conditioned Dance Generation}
FACT on AIST++~\cite{li2021aist} established large-scale music-conditioned 3D dance generation, and Bailando~\cite{siyao2022bailando} introduced discrete choreographic memory with an autoregressive dance prior. EDGE~\cite{tseng2023edge} uses Jukebox features~\cite{dhariwal2020jukebox} and Transformer diffusion for editable long-form dance, while FineDance~\cite{li2023finedance} provides fine-grained full-body choreography data. LODGE~\cite{li2024lodge} organizes long dance through coarse-to-fine primitives; MEGADance~\cite{yang2025megadance} and OpenDance~\cite{zhang2026opendance} extend genre control and multimodal choreography. These systems establish strong offline generation and data foundations. Our target is the complementary deployment problem in which a native robot-motion prefix is committed before the next causal plan is produced.

\subsection{Learning from Generated History}
Scheduled Sampling~\cite{bengio2015scheduled}, Professor Forcing~\cite{lamb2016professor}, and Diffusion Forcing~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing} address the mismatch between teacher-provided training histories and generated inference histories. MotionStreamer~\cite{xiao2025motionstreamer} and MotionStream~\cite{jiang2025motionstream} extend causal generation to streaming motion, while DiscoForcing~\cite{ji2026discoforcing} combines causal music encoding with diffusion-forcing schedules for real-time character control. These methods establish causal tokenization and streaming generation. \cof{} targets a distinct robot-state transition: the committed latent prefix is decoded into native reference motion, that motion reconstructs the next boundary state, and the following plan is learned from this generated history--state pair. The supervision therefore includes the physical consequence of commitment, not only a generated token prefix.

\subsection{Factorized Motion and Humanoid Generation}
VQ-VAE~\cite{vandenOord2017vqvae} provides the standard learned discrete codebook. T2M-GPT~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt} and MoMask~\cite{guo2024momask} use quantized motion tokens, whereas MLD~\cite{chen2023mld} models motion in a continuous latent space. DisCoRD~\cite{cho2025discord} connects discrete tokens to continuous flow decoding. Most closely, DC-Motion~\cite{wang2026dcmotion} separates human-motion structure from detail through discrete and continuous components. Our contribution is not the decomposition alone: we turn it into a robot-native streaming planning interface. Robot-specific structural supervision assigns each component a measurable role, intervention audits test that role directly, and the decoded combination determines the boundary state from which the next causal plan begins.

Physics-guided motion generation and humanoid tracking connect kinematic references to executable behavior. PhysDiff~\cite{yuan2023physdiff} injects physical projection into motion diffusion; PHC~\cite{luo2023phc}, ExBody2~\cite{ji2024exbody2}, KungfuBot~\cite{xie2026kungfubot}, BeyondMimic~\cite{liao2025beyondmimic}, and SONIC~\cite{luo2026sonic} develop scalable whole-body tracking and control. RoboPerform~\cite{li2026roboperform} directly maps audio to humanoid locomotion. \method{} instead generates persistent robot-native references under a strict causal commit contract, leaving low-level tracking to the execution layer.

\subsection{Anticipatory Music Representation}
Live Music Models~\cite{gdmlyria2025live} introduce a causal architecture for continuous music generation. Magenta RealTime~2~\cite{magenta2026mrt2} combines the SpectroStream codec~\cite{li2025spectrostream} with a frame-autoregressive music model. \method{} repurposes this model as a forecaster rather than an audio renderer: prefix states summarize observed music, while a short autoregressive rollout represents a likely future. This creates anticipatory motion conditioning without access to future waveform samples.

\section{Problem Formulation}
% ================================================================
Let $\hat{x}_{<t}$ be the G1 motion already committed at time $t$, $b_t$ the boundary state derived from the final committed frames, $m_t^{\mathrm{obs}}$ the music state encoded from arrived audio, and $m_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$ a future state sampled from the causal music model using only that arrived prefix. The generator samples a future reference horizon
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_{t:t+H-1} \sim p_\theta\!\left(
    x_{t:t+H-1}\mid \hat{x}_{<t},b_t,
    m_t^{\mathrm{obs}},m_t^{\mathrm{pred}}\right).
    \label{eq:causal_objective}
\end{equation}
Only the first $C<H$ latent steps are committed. The unused suffix is discarded, the committed prefix updates the latent history and boundary state, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal_objective} is evaluated again.

The streaming contract is expressed in reader-facing time units:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 4.27~s of recently committed motion history;
    \item a 0.53-s future plan containing 16 G1 frames;
    \item execution of the first 0.27~s, or eight frames, before replanning; and
    \item zero future-motion or future-waveform lookahead in deployable routes.
\end{itemize}

\section{A Factorized Motion Codec for Planning}
% ================================================================
The codec is not merely a compression front end. It defines the language in which future motion is planned: a structural token selects the organization of the movement, a continuous residual restores variation within that structure, and the robot boundary state anchors both components to the pose from which execution continues.
\subsection{Native Motion and Boundary State}
The decoder predicts a 34-D G1 motion vector at 30~Hz,
\begin{equation}
    x_t = [\Delta p^{\mathrm{local}}_{xy},\; h,\;
    \sin \Delta\psi,\;\cos \Delta\psi,\;q^{1:29}],
    \label{eq:g1_motion}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta p^{\mathrm{local}}_{xy}$ is heading-local planar displacement, $h$ is root height, $\Delta\psi$ is yaw change, and $q^{1:29}$ are G1 joint angles. Global planar position and absolute yaw are reconstructed by a deterministic $\mathrm{SE}(2)$ accumulator.

The compact robot boundary state is
\begin{equation}
    b_t=[h,\;v^{\mathrm{local}}_{xy},\;v_z,\;\dot{\psi},\;
    q^{1:29},\;\dot{q}^{1:29}].
    \label{eq:boundary_state}
\end{equation}
It contains the pose and first-order motion required to continue the sequence. Planar and yaw acceleration remain evaluation signals rather than network inputs. The state is recomputed from the committed motion and detached before the next planning cycle.

\subsection{Discrete Structure and Continuous Detail}
The encoder maps a 16-frame segment and its boundary state to an eight-step 16-D latent. Each step is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
    z_t=e(q_t)+r_t,
    \qquad q_t\in\{1,\ldots,512\},\quad r_t\in\mathbb{R}^{16},
    \label{eq:dc_latent}
\end{equation}
where $e(q_t)$ is a codebook vector and $r_t$ is its continuous residual. A state-conditioned decoder $D_\psi(z_t,b_t)$ reconstructs two native G1 frames. The discrete and residual streams are parallel components of one latent; neither is decoded or committed with a different horizon.

\subsection{Robot-Specific Projectors}
We supervise the representation through deterministic views of the predicted native motion. The full projector $\Phi_f$ contains normalized native motion, boundary-aware velocities, current-root-relative positions of all 29 non-root links and two sole points, and their velocities. The structural projector $\Phi_s\subset\Phi_f$ retains root, leg, and waist channels together with torso, wrist, and sole geometry. This target concentrates the discrete stream on support, root movement, coarse pose, and endpoint organization while leaving complementary articulation to the residual.

For encoder output $z$, quantized embedding $e$, and residual $r=z-\mathrm{sg}(e)$, the training reconstructions are
\begin{align}
    x_{\mathrm{full}} &=D_\psi(\mathrm{sg}(e)+r,b),\\
    x_{q} &=D_\psi(z+\mathrm{sg}(e-z),b),\\
    x_{\mathrm{robust}} &=D_\psi(\mathrm{sg}(e)+\mathcal{C}(r),b),
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{sg}$ stops gradients and $\mathcal{C}$ either removes the residual or adds small channel-scaled noise. The objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{full}}+
    \mathcal{L}_{q\text{-}\mathrm{structure}}+
    0.25\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res\text{-}robust}}+
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VQ}}.
    \label{eq:codec_loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{full}}$ is the mean squared error between the normalized blocks of $\Phi_f(x_{\mathrm{full}})$ and $\Phi_f(x)$. Both $\mathcal{L}_{q\text{-}\mathrm{structure}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res\text{-}robust}}$ compare the corresponding normalized blocks of $\Phi_s$ with the target structural view. Finally, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VQ}}$ is the codebook loss plus a 0.25-weighted commitment loss. Averaging each normalized block before combining them prevents high-dimensional geometry from dominating the native-motion terms.

\subsection{Measuring Factorization}
We audit the division of labor by swapping latent components between different test plans. Let $\Delta_q$ be structural displacement after replacing $q$ while retaining $r$, and $\Delta_r$ the displacement after replacing $r$ while retaining $q$. We define
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SD}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Delta_q]}{\mathbb{E}[\Delta_r]}.
    \label{eq:structure_dominance}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{SD}>1$ means the discrete stream has the larger effect on the declared structural view. We pair this measure with the detail recovered by the residual, within-token residual variation, effective residual rank, and noise sensitivity so that a high ratio cannot be obtained by collapsing either branch.

\section{Learning to Continue with Commit Forcing}
% ================================================================
\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/commit-forcing-generated-history.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Commit Forcing learns continuation from generated history.} A stop-gradient pass produces a deployment-form motion prefix, updated history, and reconstructed robot state. The following plan is then learned from that generated context. Residual rebasing preserves the target full motion when the generated structural token differs from the target token.}
    \label{fig:commit_forcing}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Factorized Planner}
A causal Transformer autoregressively predicts eight future structural tokens. Conditioned on the sampled structure, committed history, and boundary state, a continuous diffusion branch jointly predicts the corresponding eight residuals. The residual model uses $x_0$ prediction and DDIM sampling~\cite{song2021ddim} with ten function evaluations. Decoding produces a 0.53-s plan of 16 G1 frames.

\subsection{Receding-Horizon Commit}
At cycle $t$, the eight-step latent plan is
\begin{equation}
    P_t=\{(q_{t+i},r_{t+i})\}_{i=0}^{7}.
\end{equation}
The system executes the first four latent steps,
\begin{equation}
    P_t^{\mathrm{commit}}=\{(q_{t+i},r_{t+i})\}_{i=0}^{3},
    \label{eq:commit_prefix}
\end{equation}
decodes the eight committed motion frames, updates the recent history, and constructs the next boundary state using Eq.~\eqref{eq:boundary_state}. The two latent components advance together, preserving the codec's structural-detail decomposition while making the state transition used for replanning explicit.

\subsection{Commit Forcing}
\cof{} aligns training with this generated-history transition, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:commit_forcing}. A first forward pass samples a deployment-form plan $\tilde{P}_t$. Gradients are stopped through the sample, the executed prefix is decoded, and the same causal state update used at inference produces
\begin{equation}
    (\tilde{h}_{t+C},\tilde{b}_{t+C})
    =\mathcal{U}(h_t,b_t,\tilde{P}_t^{\mathrm{commit}}).
    \label{eq:generated_context}
\end{equation}
A second forward pass learns the following horizon from this generated context. Let $z_i^*=e(q_i^*)+r_i^*$ be the target full latent and $\tilde q_i$ the structural token supplied by the generated context. The rebased residual target is
\begin{equation}
    \tilde r_i^*=z_i^*-e(\tilde q_i),
    \qquad e(\tilde q_i)+\tilde r_i^*=z_i^*.
    \label{eq:residual_rebasing}
\end{equation}
This preserves the target full motion while expressing its detail relative to the structural token the planner will actually receive. The central idea is that continuation is learned from the consequence of a model-generated plan---decoded motion, updated history, and reconstructed robot state---rather than from another clean ground-truth window.

\section{Anticipating Music without Future Audio}
% ================================================================
A causal dancer needs both reaction and preparation. The observed-music state answers what has just happened; the predicted-future music state estimates what is likely to happen during the next motion plan. The latter is produced entirely from the arrived prefix and therefore supplies anticipation without violating causality.

Arrived audio is converted into discrete music tokens by SpectroStream, after which a frozen Magenta RealTime~2 temporal model provides the two conditions. The \emph{observed-music state} summarizes hidden states produced by the real audio prefix. The \emph{predicted-future music state} is obtained by rolling the same causal model forward far enough to cover the next short motion plan. Both states are aligned to motion time before entering the planner.

The structural planner receives reactive and future states through cross-attention. The residual generator receives time-aware pooled conditions through FiLM~\cite{perez2018film}. Each branch has an exact-null bypass, so disabling music recovers the motion parent without changing its random state or commit history. Audio inference runs asynchronously: the motion loop consumes the newest completed immutable snapshot at or before the current cutoff and records its age, rather than serializing a large music model inside the 0.27-s replanning path.

\section{Experiments}
% ================================================================
The experiments follow the method's three-part story. The codec audit asks whether structure and detail have acquired distinct, non-collapsed roles. The streaming study asks whether the complete generated-history system remains coherent over a minute. The music-interface study asks whether future states can be produced causally and within the planning budget; its effect on choreography remains the explicit final experiment.

\subsection{Protocol}
We train on the canonical FineDance-to-G1 pipeline~\cite{li2023finedance} at 30~Hz. Codec comparisons use two matched training seeds. The complete streaming study crosses two codec seeds with two downstream generator seeds; each chain is evaluated with three sampling seeds on 13 held-out sources. This produces 156 strict-causal 60-s rollouts with 4.27~s of history, a 0.53-s future plan, and a 0.27-s executed prefix. All reported physical metrics are computed from native motion through the same G1 forward-kinematics implementation.

Following the distributional-quality, diversity, and physical-plausibility evaluation families used in dance generation~\cite{li2021aist,li2023finedance}, the current result reports distribution distance, motion diversity, foot--ground plausibility, long-horizon completion, and generation latency. Because the output is native G1 motion rather than SMPL motion, the available distribution and diversity values are computed in a fixed robot-motion feature space. Factorization interventions and boundary smoothness are reported separately as mechanism-specific diagnostics. Qualitative review includes representative and difficult 60-s renders rather than selected short clips.

\subsection{Factorization Audit}
This experiment tests the codec's defining claim: changing the structural token should reorganize motion more strongly than changing the residual, while the residual must still retain meaningful variation. Figure~\ref{fig:codec_evidence} reports the matched intervention audit. Both independent seeds place the 95\% confidence interval for structure dominance above one. The residual contributes 45.4\% complementary-detail gain, retains 76.6\% within-token variation, and uses 15.15 of 16 effective dimensions.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/codec-factorization-evidence.pdf}
    \caption{\textbf{Intervention evidence for the factorized motion codec.} (a) Replacing the structural token changes the declared structural motion view more strongly than replacing the residual for both training seeds; error bars are paired-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals. (b) Complementary-detail gain, within-token variation, and effective rank show that the continuous residual remains informative rather than collapsing.}
    \label{fig:codec_evidence}
\end{figure*}

For the compact-state representation, the mean structural displacements from discrete and residual swaps are 0.5916 and 0.4182. Removing the residual increases structural error by approximately 43\% and complementary-detail error by approximately 83\%. Adding 3\% channel-scaled residual noise changes structural and detail error by 0.12\% and 0.40\%, respectively. Together, these results show that the discrete stream controls the declared structure while the residual carries stable, complementary variation.

\subsection{Long-Horizon Streaming}
This experiment tests whether the codec and generated-history training loop remain coherent beyond a single planning window. All four crossed training chains complete structural-token learning, residual refinement, and \cof{} training, including exact continuation after interruption. Across the twelve canonical evaluation suites, every rollout remains finite and upright, with no inversion or tilt above $30^\circ$. Table~\ref{tab:streaming} reports the canonical median together with typical replanning latency. These results validate the integrated motion system; the isolated effect of \cof{} remains assigned to the TODO ablation in Sec.~V-C.

\subsection{Music-Interface Systems Validation}
This experiment tests whether future-music prediction is genuinely causal and computationally usable before asking whether it improves dance. The interface passes prefix equivalence, post-cutoff mutation invariance, deterministic save/restore, future-hidden alignment, and exact-null motion tests for both the 230M and 2.4B Magenta RealTime~2 models. On GH200 hardware, steady predicted-state throughput is 75.7 frames/s for the 230M model and 17.8 frames/s for the 2.4B model. Since synchronous refresh would require 101.25 frames/s, the system uses the asynchronous snapshot interface shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. This preserves the motion replanning deadline while exposing snapshot age as an explicit runtime measurement.

\section{Discussion}
% ================================================================
The completed evidence establishes two links of the proposed causal chain. The factorized codec has a measured structural-detail division of labor, and the complete codec--\cof{} motion system remains coherent in strict-causal minute-long generation. The factorization audit is decisive because it measures intervention effects rather than assigning semantic roles from reconstruction loss alone. The long rollouts establish the capability of the integrated motion foundation; the matched history ablation will identify the specific contribution of \cof{}.

The generated boundary state is reconstructed from committed robot-native reference motion, making the transition used by the next plan explicit. This paper studies reference generation under that causal contract; controller-in-the-loop execution is assigned to the next evaluation layer.

The future-music interface completes the architectural story but not yet the empirical one. It supplies a causal, latency-aware path from arrived audio to both structural and detail generation. The decisive remaining test is whether the predicted-future state improves music--motion correspondence over observed-only conditioning while preserving the motion, support, diversity, and long-horizon properties established here.

\section{Conclusion}
% ================================================================
We presented \method{} as a three-part route from offline choreography to streaming humanoid dance. A factorized motion codec supplies a verified structural vocabulary and complementary detail channel. \cof{} continues planning from generated motion history and its reconstructed robot state. A causal music model supplies both observed and predicted-future states, giving the planner a mechanism for anticipation without future-waveform access. Multi-seed intervention audits and 156 one-minute rollouts establish a stable robot-native motion foundation, while causal and runtime tests establish that future-music prediction can enter the same streaming loop. \method{} therefore provides a unified basis for listening, anticipating, committing motion, and continuing from its consequences.
