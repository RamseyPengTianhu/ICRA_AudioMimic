\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Observed and Predicted Music Conditions}
Music-to-dance models combine acoustic descriptors~\cite{mcfee2015librosa} or
learned features~\cite{li2023mert,zhu2025muq} with choreography models.
FACT and Bailando model coordinated sequences and choreographic
memory~\cite{li2021aist,siyao2022bailando}; EDGE and LODGE develop diffusion-based
and long-form generation~\cite{tseng2023edge,li2024lodge}. FineDance,
MEGADance, and SoulNet address fine-grained motion, genre, and music--motion
alignment~\cite{li2023finedance,yang2025megadance,li2025soulnet}.

DiscoForcing~\cite{ji2026discoforcing} uses a task-specific causal VQ-PAE
encoder to describe heard audio through rhythmic tokens and periodic alignment.
We instead condition on explicit future states from a generative music prior.
MRT-2 is pretrained on approximately 71,000 hours of
music~\cite{magenta2026mrt2}, providing continuation knowledge beyond our paired
dance training. Foundation-model features already support dance in
EDGE through Jukebox~\cite{tseng2023edge,dhariwal2020jukebox} and in
DGFM~\cite{liu2025dgfm}; earlier robot systems also predict beat
timing~\cite{nakahara2009tempo}. Our distinction is the use of generative
music-state forecasts under a causal audio constraint. Live Music
Models~\cite{gdmlyria2025live} and MRT-2 supply the music-generation foundation;
we transfer its predictions to structured streaming motion.

\subsection{Structure--Detail Representation and Training}
Discrete tokens support motion generation in T2M-GPT and
MoMask~\cite{vandenOord2017vqvae,zhang2023t2mgpt,guo2024momask}; MLD uses
continuous latent diffusion~\cite{chen2023mld}, and DisCoRD uses flow-based
decoding from discrete tokens~\cite{cho2025discord}. Most closely,
DC-Motion~\cite{wang2026dcmotion} combines discrete structure with continuous
residuals. We build on this hybrid principle, using designated robot-kinematic
supervision to give the components complementary roles through a shared,
boundary-conditioned decoder. Neural Categorical Priors and GPC learn
vocabularies for physical control~\cite{zhu2023ncp,shi2026gpc}; our latents
instead decode into explicit motion references for a separate tracker.

\subsection{Continuation from Generated Context}
Scheduled Sampling and Professor Forcing address training--sampling
mismatch~\cite{bengio2015scheduled,lamb2016professor}. Diffusion Forcing varies
per-token noise~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing}; MotionStream and MotionStreamer
develop causal motion generation~\cite{jiang2025motionstream,xiao2025motionstreamer}.
MotionStreamer's Two-Forward strategy uses first-pass predictions in second-pass
history. \cof{} constructs the joint structure--detail history--state transition: sampled
components enter history together, and their decoded committed motion determines the
next reference state. Its distinction is this coupled update. Diffusion Policy
and Real-Time Chunking address committed actions during plan
updates~\cite{chi2023diffusionpolicy,black2025rtc}; CoF constructs the context
under which continuation is learned.

\paragraph{Robot execution}
Reusable trackers support expressive motion~\cite{luo2023phc,ji2024exbody2,liao2025beyondmimic,luo2026sonic},
while HARMON and RoboPerform introduce language and audio
conditioning~\cite{jiang2025harmon,li2026roboperform}. PhysDiff, terrain-aware
reference generation, and PARC incorporate physical correction or
adaptation~\cite{yuan2023physdiff,zhang2026wholebody,xu2025parc}.
Our fixed-controller interface keeps reference generation separate from
measured-state feedback, allowing their outputs to be examined independently.
