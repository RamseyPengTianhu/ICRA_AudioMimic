\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Music Conditioning and Dance Generation}
Music-conditioned motion uses both explicit acoustic descriptors, such as
rhythmic and spectral features extracted with librosa~\cite{mcfee2015librosa},
and learned representations such as MERT~\cite{li2023mert} and
MuQ~\cite{zhu2025muq}. AIST++/FACT~\cite{li2021aist} establishes a large-scale
foundation for music-conditioned choreography, and
Bailando~\cite{siyao2022bailando} uses discrete choreographic memory.
EDGE~\cite{tseng2023edge} develops diffusion-based editable dance,
LODGE~\cite{li2024lodge} addresses long-form generation, and
FineDance~\cite{li2023finedance} supports fine-grained full-body choreography.
MEGADance~\cite{yang2025megadance} explores genre-aware generation, while
SoulNet~\cite{li2025soulnet} combines hierarchical motion modeling with
music--motion alignment. These works motivate evaluating musical correspondence
together with motion quality, rather than treating either as sufficient alone.

Streaming additionally constrains which audio is available at each decision.
DiscoForcing~\cite{ji2026discoforcing} combines causal audio encoding with
diffusion forcing for streaming character control and humanoid deployment.
Encoding heard music and predicting upcoming music are distinct operations.
Generative music models, including Jukebox~\cite{dhariwal2020jukebox} and
Live Music Models~\cite{gdmlyria2025live}, provide foundations for continuation
from musical context. We use frozen Magenta RealTime~2~\cite{magenta2026mrt2}
forecasts as prospective conditions for robot motion. This supplies inferred
musical lookahead without accessing the future recording; the forecaster itself
is not a contribution of this work.

\subsection{Motion Representations and Motor Priors}
Vector quantization~\cite{vandenOord2017vqvae} supports discrete motion models
such as T2M-GPT~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt} and MoMask~\cite{guo2024momask}, whereas
MLD~\cite{chen2023mld} generates motion through continuous latent diffusion.
DisCoRD~\cite{cho2025discord} reconstructs motion from discrete tokens through
rectified-flow decoding. Most closely, DC-Motion~\cite{wang2026dcmotion}
combines discrete structure and continuous residuals for human motion generation.
Our design builds on this hybrid representation principle, adding designated
robot-kinematic supervision to discrete and residual-corrupted reconstructions
and conditioning the shared decoder on the reference boundary state.
The distinction concerns the learned kinematic roles and streaming use of the
components, not the discrete--continuous split alone.

Motion vocabularies can also provide interfaces to physical skills.
Neural Categorical Priors~\cite{zhu2023ncp} learn discrete priors for
physics-based character control. GPC~\cite{shi2026gpc} jointly learns an
FSQ-based vocabulary and a control policy through reinforcement learning,
then models code sequences autoregressively. In contrast to latents decoded
into control actions, our D+C latents decode into explicit robot motion
references for a separate tracker. This permits representation interventions
and reference--execution comparisons without retraining that controller.

\subsection{Streaming Generation and Generated Context}
Recursive generation encounters histories different from those supplied during
teacher-forced training. Scheduled Sampling~\cite{bengio2015scheduled}
substitutes model predictions for observed inputs, Professor
Forcing~\cite{lamb2016professor} aligns recurrent training and sampling dynamics,
and Diffusion Forcing~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing} trains with independently
varying per-token noise levels. MotionStream~\cite{jiang2025motionstream}
develops causal motion tokenization. MotionStreamer~\cite{xiao2025motionstreamer}
combines causal compression with diffusion-based autoregression; its Two-Forward
strategy replaces a subset of ground-truth latents with first-pass predictions
for a second training pass.

\cof{} addresses the complete reference transition in a boundary-conditioned
D+C generator. Generated structure and residuals enter history together, and
their decoded prefix determines the next boundary state. Its distinguishing
operation is therefore joint history--state construction, rather than the use
of two forward passes alone. Receding-horizon execution in Diffusion
Policy~\cite{chi2023diffusionpolicy} and asynchronous chunk updating in
Real-Time Chunking~\cite{black2025rtc} concern a related but different issue:
preserving actions committed for execution while updating a plan. \cof{}
instead constructs the context under which continuation is learned.

\subsection{Humanoid Motion Generation and Execution}
PHC~\cite{luo2023phc} develops reusable tracking and recovery for simulated
avatars, while ExBody2~\cite{ji2024exbody2},
BeyondMimic~\cite{liao2025beyondmimic}, and SONIC~\cite{luo2026sonic} advance
expressive and versatile humanoid control. Higher-level conditioning extends
these capabilities: HARMON~\cite{jiang2025harmon} generates whole-body motion
from language, and RoboPerform~\cite{li2026roboperform} uses a teacher--student
framework to combine motion content with audio-derived style. Together with
DiscoForcing's streaming motion-conversion and tracking pipeline, these systems
establish complementary routes from external conditions to embodied behavior.

Generation and control can also be coupled through physical correction.
PhysDiff~\cite{yuan2023physdiff} introduces simulator-based correction into
motion generation. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026wholebody} generate terrain-aware
robot references and fine-tune their tracker with a frozen generator, while
PARC~\cite{xu2025parc} uses tracked motions to iteratively augment the generator's
training data. Our framework instead retains a fixed downstream controller and
learns continuation within the generated reference process. Physical feedback
closes the controller loop, not the generator's reference-history update.
This separation motivates measuring musical suitability and motion quality
in both references and execution, alongside tracking accuracy.
