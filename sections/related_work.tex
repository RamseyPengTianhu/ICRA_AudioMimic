\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Learning-Based Humanoid Control and Motion Tracking}

Reinforcement learning and motion imitation provide foundations
for expressive whole-body control.
Neural Categorical Priors~\cite{zhu2023ncp} and Generative
Pretrained Controllers~\cite{shi2026gpc} connect learned
motion vocabularies with physics-based character control.
Reference-conditioned policies further enable reusable
motion tracking: PHC~\cite{luo2023phc} demonstrates tracking
and recovery in simulated avatars, while
ExBody2~\cite{ji2024exbody2},
BeyondMimic~\cite{liao2025beyondmimic}, and
SONIC~\cite{luo2026sonic} advance expressive and versatile
humanoid control.
These methods establish how reference movement can be
realized under physical dynamics.
Our work uses a pretrained whole-body controller as the
execution layer and focuses on generating the music-conditioned
references it follows.
The controller retains measured-state feedback, while the
generator maintains its own committed reference context.

\subsection{Music-Conditioned Motion Generation}

Music-to-dance systems use explicit acoustic descriptors,
such as those extracted with librosa~\cite{mcfee2015librosa},
or learned audio representations from models including
Jukebox~\cite{dhariwal2020jukebox},
MERT~\cite{li2023mert}, and MuQ~\cite{zhu2025muq}.
AIST++/FACT~\cite{li2021aist} and
Bailando~\cite{siyao2022bailando} develop music-conditioned
choreography through sequence modeling and discrete motion
representations.
EDGE~\cite{tseng2023edge} and LODGE~\cite{li2024lodge}
advance diffusion-based and long-form dance generation,
while FineDance~\cite{li2023finedance},
MEGADance~\cite{yang2025megadance}, and
SoulNet~\cite{li2025soulnet} address fine-grained motion,
genre-aware generation, and music--motion alignment.

Streaming generation additionally constrains the audio
available before each motion decision.
DiscoForcing~\cite{ji2026discoforcing} uses causal audio
conditioning and diffusion forcing for streaming motion
and humanoid deployment.
Our music interface supplies explicit forecasts of upcoming
music states from the observed prefix.
Generative music models, including Live Music
Models~\cite{gdmlyria2025live} and
MRT-2~\cite{magenta2026mrt2}, provide the
continuation prior used for this purpose.
We investigate how their predicted states can condition
upcoming robot motion without access to future waveform
samples.
The music forecaster remains frozen; paired dance training
learns how to use its predictions.

\subsection{Motion Representations and Generation}

Discrete motion models such as T2M-GPT~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt}
and MoMask~\cite{guo2024momask} use quantized vocabularies,
while MLD~\cite{chen2023mld} generates motion through
continuous latent diffusion.
DisCoRD~\cite{cho2025discord} connects discrete tokens
with continuous decoding.
Most closely, DC-Motion~\cite{wang2026dcmotion} combines
discrete structure and continuous residuals.
Our D+C design builds on this hybrid principle through
robot-kinematic reconstruction objectives that assign
designated structural responsibilities to discrete codes,
with full motion reconstructed through a shared
boundary-conditioned decoder.

Recursive generation also introduces a mismatch between
recorded training context and generated inference context.
Scheduled Sampling~\cite{bengio2015scheduled} and
Diffusion Forcing~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing} address
related sequence-training problems.
MotionStreamer~\cite{xiao2025motionstreamer} combines
causal motion compression with diffusion-based autoregression
and Two-Forward training.
\cof{} focuses on the transition induced by our D+C
generator: sampled structure and detail enter the committed
history together, and their decoded motion determines
the next reference boundary state.
Its distinguishing operation is the joint construction of
this history--state pair for continuation training.

\subsection{Integrating Motion Generation with Humanoid Control}

Connecting conditional generation to physical control
enables humanoids to express higher-level intent.
HARMON~\cite{jiang2025harmon} generates whole-body humanoid
motion from language descriptions.
RoboPerform~\cite{li2026roboperform} combines motion
content with audio-derived style through a teacher--student
control framework, while
DiscoForcing~\cite{ji2026discoforcing} connects streaming
motion generation to humanoid execution through retargeting
and tracking.
These systems establish complementary interfaces between
external conditions, generated movement, and robot control.

Other approaches use physical execution to inform generation
or adapt the controller.
PhysDiff~\cite{yuan2023physdiff} incorporates physics-based
correction into denoising, and
PARC~\cite{xu2025parc} uses physically tracked motion
to augment generator training.
BeyondMimic~\cite{liao2025beyondmimic} extends tracking
to guided generative motion composition, while
terrain-aware humanoid generation~\cite{zhang2026wholebody}
couples robot-native references with tracker adaptation.
Our framework performs retargeting during data preparation,
generates robot-native references online, and delivers
committed motion to fixed SONIC control.
Continuation training operates within the generated reference
process.
We therefore distinguish reference generation, delivery,
and measured execution, and assess musical correspondence
and motion quality alongside tracking accuracy.
