\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{Soundtrack correspondence and motion quality}
Across the 198 generated references, the frozen evaluator favors intended
music over the other gallery tracks under both startup settings. Positive
margins in every generator training seed support correspondence beyond one
sampled model. The motion-quality study addresses complementary properties:
history corruption improves distribution distances most under mixed-start
training, while contact and jerk favor clean history. Musical matching,
distributional fidelity, and smoothness should therefore be assessed together.

\paragraph{Evidence scope and evaluator selection}
The primary results rescore fixed generated motions in an eleven-track gallery;
the complete available gallery and all training/sampling repeats are retained.
The evaluator configuration was selected after inspection of a 30-content-group
calibration cohort. Its calibration numbers and fixed-gallery bootstrap
intervals are descriptive, rather than selection-independent confirmation of
configuration superiority. The score has not been validated against human
preference or executed trajectories. The 18-track motion-quality study is a
separate cohort, and the illustrated sequence is a selected qualitative case.

Rescoring fixed motion establishes soundtrack correspondence, while matched
music-input interventions are needed to isolate conditional adaptation and
the incremental benefit of forecasts over heard-music features. General music
pretraining supplies a prior beyond the paired dance corpus; improved
out-of-distribution dance generalization remains a hypothesis requiring
matched evaluation. Matched
representation and Teacher-Forcing/Commit-Forcing studies are likewise needed
to attribute component gains. These distinctions keep the current reference
results separate from causal method comparisons and physical execution.

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced \method{}, transferring a frozen music foundation model's
causal forecasts to robot-native
structure--detail generation and \cof{} continuation training. A frozen
G1-native music--motion evaluator favors the intended soundtrack in 74.20\%
and 75.01\% of pairwise comparisons for two startup variants on a fixed
collection of 60\,s references, with positive score margins across all three
generator training seeds. These results provide quantitative soundtrack
correspondence evidence within the structured streaming framework.
The reference interface connects the resulting motion to fixed whole-body
control, enabling subsequent comparisons of generation and execution.
