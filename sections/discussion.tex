\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:evidence_scope}
\paragraph{Supported findings and attribution}
The completed 198-trajectory study supports soundtrack correspondence within
the fixed gallery. Isolating the benefit of anticipation requires matched
forecast-versus-heard conditioning; attributing representation and continuation
gains requires independently trained structure-only, detail-only, and combined-representation comparisons and
CoF-versus-TF rollouts. These comparisons remain pending. Broader music
pretraining supplies a transferable prediction prior, while improved
out-of-distribution dance generalization remains a hypothesis. The historical
replay diagnostics characterize tracking separately from these current
reference-generation results.

\paragraph{Evaluation scope and selection}
All available gallery tracks and training/sampling repeats are retained.
The evaluator configuration was selected after inspection of a 30-content-group
calibration cohort; its calibration scores and fixed-gallery intervals are
descriptive, not selection-independent evidence of configuration superiority.
The score has not been validated against human preference or executed
trajectories. The motion-quality cohort is separate, and the illustrated
sequence is a selected qualitative case. Its complete source and selection
record are retained with the evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\method{} brings anticipatory music conditioning to structured streaming dance:
a frozen music foundation model predicts prospective context, kinematics-guided
structure--detail training organizes motion components, and \cof{} learns continuation after
their joint commitment. Completed evaluation finds consistent soundtrack
correspondence across generator training seeds. Together, the method and
reference interface provide an explicit framework for studying how musical
foresight translates into continuously generated robot motion.
