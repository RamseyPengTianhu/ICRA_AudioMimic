\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Our primary result tests soundtrack correspondence: does a generated dance
receive a higher matching score with its intended music than with alternative
tracks? We evaluate fixed generated references with one frozen evaluator, then
examine motion quality and the comparisons needed to isolate adaptation,
representation, and continuation.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
The main correspondence study uses 198 previously generated 60\,s FineDance
trajectories in native G1 space. Eleven source tracks are shared by two
forecast-conditioned, history-regularized startup variants, each with three
independently trained generators and three sampling seeds per generator
($11\times2\times3\times3=198$). We call the variants \emph{prefix-aware startup}
and \emph{direct-prefix startup}. They instantiate the same coupled generation
framework; the table characterizes each variant without selecting a startup winner.
Each trajectory contains 1,800 frames at 30\,Hz. The eleven associated audio
tracks form one fixed gallery, with source-specific audio offsets preserved.

Generator training uses 6,250 updates with batch size 48. Mixed-start training
draws 20\% song-origin starts and 80\% continuation windows; continuation-start
training uses ordinary windows throughout. Model and sampler details follow
Section~\ref{sec:dc} and Appendix~\ref{app:training}. A separate motion-quality
study uses the complete archived 18-track test cohort, three training seeds,
three sampling seeds, and common source-supported 23\,s segments beginning at
frame 264. Its distribution and contact scores are not pooled with the
60\,s correspondence results. The downstream SONIC controller is fixed.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation_protocol}
\paragraph{Music--motion matching}
Motivated by music--motion retrieval~\cite{li2025soulnet}, we use a frozen
G1-native evaluator trained on paired reference music and motion independently
of the generator. The adopted score uses five-second posterior-mean cosine
similarity, standard content-group sampling during evaluator training, and an
ensemble of three final evaluator checkpoints. We average their scores before
comparing pairs. The 357-dimensional motion frontend preserves full root
orientation and uses training-only normalization. This G1 adaptation and cosine
readout are specified explicitly in Appendix~\ref{app:metric_construction};
they are not an off-the-shelf human-body evaluation checkpoint.

Let $s_w(x,a)$ be the ensemble cosine score for motion $x$ and audio $a$ in
window $w$. Higher is better. Each 60\,s motion is divided into 56 five-second
windows with one-second stride. For intended audio $a_i$ and another gallery
track $a_j$, we measure
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{ijw}=s_w(x_i,a_i)-s_w(x_i,a_j).
 \label{eq:mmr_margin}
\end{equation}
The \emph{pairwise correspondence accuracy} is the percentage of these
comparisons in which $\delta_{ijw}>0$, assigning half credit to ties. The
reported margin averages $\delta_{ijw}$. Thus the score asks whether the
intended soundtrack is favored over an alternative while the motion is fixed;
random pairwise ordering gives 50\%. This is a two-way preference statistic,
not eleven-way retrieval accuracy or a percentage of overall dance quality.

\paragraph{Dance quality and adaptation}
Kinetic and geometric Fr\'echet distances (FID$_k$, FID$_g$) compare motion
features with reference distributions; diversity (Div$_k$, Div$_g$) uses the
same feature spaces. Joint jerk, activity, and root/contact proxies complement
these distribution measures. Beat Alignment Score (BAS), event coverage, and
onset--motion correlation characterize rhythm. Music-input interventions test
a different question from rescoring fixed motion: whether changing the input
changes the generated dance appropriately. Primary matching scores retain the
audio timeline; lag alignment is reserved for tracking diagnostics.

\paragraph{Aggregation}
All ten wrong-track donors and all 56 windows contribute to each motion's
result. Sampling repeats are averaged within each generator-training-seed and
query-track cell, followed by equal averaging across the three seeds and
11 tracks. Descriptive 95\% intervals use 10,000 bootstrap draws over training
seeds and query tracks, with the observed music gallery fixed. Overlapping
windows and reused donors are not counted as independent samples.
Appendix~\ref{app:scope} gives the exact aggregation and evaluator calibration.

The result supports soundtrack correspondence for this collection of generated
references. Because the dance is held fixed when music donors are substituted,
the comparison does not by itself isolate a causal benefit of forecast
conditioning. That requires the controlled-generation comparisons below.

\paragraph{Complementary motion-quality evidence}
The separate 18-track study examines the effect of full-history corruption
(FHC). Under mixed-start training, kinetic/geometric FID changes from
208.599/0.482 with clean history to 162.831/0.410 with FHC, with the same
favorable direction across all training seeds. Continuation-start changes are
smaller, from 156.218/0.372 to 154.180/0.361. Mixed-start contact and jerk favor
clean history. These measurements expose a distribution--smoothness tradeoff
and complement, rather than replace, soundtrack correspondence.
Appendix~\ref{app:fhc} reports the motion-quality values.

\paragraph{Conditional specificity}
The paired intervention protocol uses correct, exact-null, same-track wrong-time,
same-genre wrong-track, cross-genre wrong-track, and $\pm4$ music-frame shifts
($\pm160$\,ms). Initial history, boundary state, and sampling seed are matched;
each rollout then advances with its own committed history. Outputs are scored
against the designated soundtrack. These inference-time interventions differ
from separately trained no-music controls.

For songs $a,b$ and generated dances $D_a,D_b$, the correct-pair advantage is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{pair}}=\tfrac12\big[
s(D_a,a)-s(D_a,b)+s(D_b,b)-s(D_b,a)\big],
\label{eq:cross_pair_advantage}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is the higher-is-better ensemble cosine score averaged over windows. Positive values favor intended
pairings; both directional effects are retained. The cross-pairing design applies
to generated and executed motion; its matched results remain to be filled.

\subsection{Online Execution and Reliability}
\label{sec:execution_results}
The execution protocol compares generated, bridge-sent, controller-consumed,
and measured trajectories. Joint RMSE, amplitude and squared-velocity retention
measure tracking, while matching, rhythm, and motion-quality scores assess the
resulting dance. Executed dataset references provide a calibration control.
Supporting tracking measurements are given in Appendix~\ref{app:execution}.

Offline--online controls separately vary condition content/age and reference
delivery, retaining source-trajectory identity and preview duration. Runtime
reporting includes valid forecast coverage, planning-time tails, deadline
misses, and stale-condition fallback. Startup and steady state are reported
separately, with all attempts included in completion statistics.
