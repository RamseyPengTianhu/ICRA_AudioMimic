\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate whether \method{} generates coherent robot-native
dance that responds appropriately to music, and whether these
properties are retained during execution.
The evaluation covers motion quality and musical adaptation,
using motion-distribution metrics, explicit beat alignment,
and learned music--motion correspondence.
Controlled ablations examine musical anticipation,
kinematics-guided D+C supervision, and \cof{}.
Streaming and execution experiments assess timing,
reliability, and changes from generated references
to tracked motion.

% ================================================================
\subsection{Setup and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
\label{sec:evaluation_protocol}

We use paired FineDance music and human motion retargeted
to the Unitree G1.
Data are partitioned by music content into training,
validation, and test sets.
The generator uses the native 38D representation defined
in the method, while evaluation features are extracted
from decoded robot motion.

Motion-quality comparisons use 23\,s segments from
18 tracks.
Long-horizon correspondence and continuation experiments
use 60\,s segments from eleven tracks.
These cohorts are reported separately.
Each generator configuration uses three independent
training seeds and three sampling seeds per track.
Methods share evaluation tracks, initial reference contexts,
soundtrack offsets, generation durations, and sampling
settings within each comparison.
During free-running generation, subsequent contexts evolve
from each model's own committed outputs.
We score the concatenated committed motion using a fixed
configuration and initialization rule across metrics.

Evaluator training and model selection exclude the music
content used for generator evaluation.
All scores use common valid audio--motion intervals and
the recorded soundtrack timing.
We report training-seed variation and 95\% confidence
intervals from song-level bootstrap resampling.
Paired comparisons resample the same songs across conditions.
Scalar scores are aggregated within songs before averaging
across songs and training seeds.
Distributional metrics are computed from pooled feature
sets and recomputed during resampling.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:evaluation_metrics}

\paragraph{Motion quality}
We report kinetic and geometric Fr\'echet distances,
FID$_k$ and FID$_g$, between generated and retargeted-reference
motion distributions.
Robot-specific kinetic features summarize FK-point speeds
and accelerations; geometric features describe root-relative
body configurations and inter-point distances.
Both populations use the same G1 model and feature extractor.
Div$_k$ and Div$_g$ measure diversity in these spaces and
are interpreted jointly with distributional fidelity.
PFC provides a foot-motion and root-acceleration-based
physical-consistency proxy.
Motion activity, jerk, and position/velocity jumps at
commitment boundaries provide additional diagnostics
of freezing, smoothness, and continuation.

\paragraph{Explicit beat alignment}
Audio beats are extracted with a fixed librosa beat tracker.
Motion beats are local minima of smoothed mean FK-point
speed.
We report both motion-to-audio and audio-to-motion
Beat Alignment Scores (BAS), using a Gaussian matching
scale of 0.10\,s.
The two directions measure proximity from the respective
event sets.
Beat F1 complements BAS through one-to-one event matching
with a 0.20\,s tolerance.
Detectors and matching parameters remain fixed across methods.

Dynamic response is additionally measured by the zero-lag
Spearman correlation between audio RMS energy and
root-relative FK-point speed, aggregated in one-second bins.
We interpret this energy--activity association alongside
motion quality and retargeted-reference responses.

\paragraph{Learned music--motion correspondence}
Following SoulNet~\cite{li2025soulnet}, we adapt a
music--motion representation-learning evaluator to
robot-native motion.
The music branch receives Jukebox
features~\cite{dhariwal2020jukebox}; the motion branch
receives root, body-pose, velocity, and contact features.
Variational encoders and a shared motion decoder are
trained with reconstruction, latent alignment,
KL regularization, and contrastive objectives.
The evaluator and training-derived normalization are
frozen before generator comparison.

For each five-second window, the score $s$ averages
audio--motion embedding cosine similarities from three
independently trained evaluator instances.
For motion $x_i$, intended music $a_i$, alternative
music $a_j$, and window $w$, we define
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{ijw}
    =
    s(x_i^{(w)},a_i^{(w)})
    -
    s(x_i^{(w)},a_j^{(w)}).
    \label{eq:mmr_margin}
\end{equation}
We report the mean correspondence margin and pairwise
accuracy, assigning half credit to ties.
Each 60\,s trajectory is evaluated with five-second
windows at a one-second stride against its intended
soundtrack and ten fixed alternatives.
Window-level margins and decisions are averaged over
windows and alternatives before aggregation across
sampling repeats, songs, and generator seeds.
The gallery and soundtrack offsets are shared across methods.

\subsection{Component Ablations}
\label{sec:component_ablations}

\paragraph{Musical anticipation}
We compare separately trained heard-only and
forecast-conditioned generators with matched motion
architectures, training budgets, tracks, and sampling settings.
A real-future condition provides an additional reference
comparison.
Motion quality, beat alignment, and learned correspondence
are evaluated over the same intervals.
These comparisons isolate the benefit of predictive
musical context from the effect of music conditioning itself.

\paragraph{Kinematics-guided D+C generation}
We compare codecs trained with and without the proposed

Full-motion and structure-only reconstruction errors

Fixed-structure residual interventions examine

Downstream generation metrics test whether this

\paragraph{Committed continuation}
We compare Teacher Forcing and \cof{} on matched
60\,s free-running generations.
A generated-history-only control retains the recorded
boundary state, isolating the effect of constructing
the generated history and its resulting state together.
We evaluate the full sequence and three consecutive
20\,s intervals, reporting musical correspondence,
motion activity, and commitment-boundary discontinuities.
Clean-history, history-dropout, and full-history-corruption
comparisons separately characterize the contribution
of history regularization.

\subsection{Streaming and Robot Execution}
\label{sec:execution_results}

We evaluate reference execution in MuJoCo using a fixed
SONIC whole-body controller.
Retargeted reference motion and generated motion pass
through the same interface.
We distinguish committed generation, delivered reference,
controller-consumed target, and measured execution.
This separation identifies changes introduced by reference
delivery and physical tracking.

\paragraph{Streaming performance}
Cached and live music conditioning are reported separately,
as are saved-trajectory replay and online reference delivery.
We measure generation latency, deadline-miss rate,
effective future-music coverage at action decisions,
and motion-reference preview at controller consumption.
All measurements use recorded timestamps.
Hardware, planning horizon, commitment length, and sampling
settings are fixed within each comparison.

\paragraph{Preservation under execution}
We compare motion quality and musical adaptation before
and after execution using the same soundtrack timeline
and common valid intervals.
Learned correspondence on executed motion is accompanied
by execution-domain evaluator checks.
Completion rate, joint tracking error, and amplitude
and squared-velocity retention characterize execution
reliability and dynamic attenuation.
Any lag-compensated tracking analysis is reported
separately from audio-synchronized musical scores.
Failed runs remain included in completion statistics.

Matched visualizations show how generated dance changes
across music inputs and how those movements are retained
during tracking.
Rendered references and measured execution are identified
separately.
