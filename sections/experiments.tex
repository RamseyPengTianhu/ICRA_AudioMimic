\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Our evaluation centers on three questions: whether forecast conditioning adds value beyond heard music, whether kinematic supervision produces complementary structure and detail, and whether learning from committed transitions improves continuation over TF. We report completed music-condition and history-training comparisons on FineDance and specify the matched component studies whose results are pending.

\subsection{Data, Controls, and Metrics}
We use FineDance motion retargeted to G1 and represented as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:motion}. The quantitative cohort comprises 18 official test tracks, with three independently trained models and three sampling seeds per model. Each track contributes the same source-supported 23\,s segment across compared methods. The 60\,s qualitative sequence is evaluated separately from this quantitative cohort. Startup training is either \emph{mixed-start} (song-origin starts mixed with ordinary windows) or \emph{continuation-start} (ordinary continuation windows).

\paragraph{Music matching.}
Our frozen G1 music--motion retrieval evaluator, inspired by SoulNet's matching formulation~\cite{li2025soulnet}, scores compatibility between audio and generated motion. Its MMR-MS distance is lower for better matching; it combines sequence-level and temporal features. The evaluator is independent of generator optimization. We report this as the primary matching measure, accompanied by beat alignment (BAS). These complementary measures assess sequence compatibility and rhythmic correspondence, respectively.

\paragraph{Motion quality and continuation.}
Kinetic and geometric Fr\'echet distances measure distribution similarity; their corresponding diversity measures describe motion variation. Root motion, contact/grounding proxies, and abrupt changes characterize properties that distribution scores do not resolve. The CoF comparison is designed to assess boundary continuity and all three non-overlapping 20\,s portions of a 60\,s rollout. Appendix~\ref{app:scope} details the paired evaluation and uncertainty estimates.

\subsection{Anticipatory Music Conditioning}
\label{sec:music_results}
\paragraph{Does the generator use its music forecast?}
A forecast is useful to the dance generator only if its information changes the resulting music--motion correspondence. Table~\ref{tab:music_use} compares forecast-conditioned training with separately trained no-music controls. Mixed-start training reduces MMR-MS by 0.782, with a 95\% interval of [0.294, 1.285]; continuation-start training reduces it by 1.123, with an interval of [0.621, 1.649]. All three training seeds agree in direction under both settings. The agreement across startup settings supports the use of forecast-derived music information beyond a particular initialization policy.

\subsection{D+C Representation and Training}
\label{sec:dc_results}
\paragraph{Complementary roles.}
The structural objective is intended to preserve body organization while allowing complementary articulation. To test this mechanism, the study design combines reconstruction in the two kinematic views with swaps of discrete and continuous codes. A stronger structural response to a discrete-code swap than to a detail-code swap would support the intended division of labor. Reconstruction quality, residual contribution, and effective rank must be considered together so that an apparently selective response is not explained by a collapsed branch. These mechanism results remain pending.

\paragraph{Generation benefit.}
Table~\ref{tab:dc} specifies the complementary generation test: separately trained discrete-only and continuous-only generators against D+C with its paired supervision. The comparison uses the same full-orientation motion representation and matched training and evaluation settings. Unlike zeroing a residual at inference, separately trained alternatives test how each representation supports generation when optimized in its own right. Results are pending.

\subsection{Effect of History Corruption}
We compare clean-history training with FHC to examine how regularizing the motion context affects the generated distribution. Under mixed-start training, FHC reduces kinetic/geometric FID from 208.599/0.482 to 162.831/0.410, with favorable directions across all training seeds. The continuation-start changes are smaller, from 156.218/0.372 to 154.180/0.361. This shows that the distribution-level effect depends on the startup policy.

The matching and physical proxies give a more specific interpretation. MMR-MS point estimates improve under both startup settings, but the paired intervals for FHC versus clean history include zero. Mixed-start contact and jerk measures favor clean history. FHC therefore changes the distribution--smoothness tradeoff; its distribution gains do not establish a separate gain in musical matching. Appendix~\ref{app:fhc} reports the complete metric values.

\subsection{Reference Execution with a Fixed Controller}
\label{sec:execution_results}
The execution interface asks whether the delivered reference retains its motion characteristics after tracking. We distinguish the generated motion, the bridge's sent reference, the controller's consumed target, and measured execution. This separates changes introduced by representation conversion and delivery from target-tracking error.

A separate MuJoCo replay study of an earlier 34-dimensional generator configuration provides supporting measurements of this interface (Appendix~\ref{app:execution}). Four previously generated trajectories were replayed through the same fixed SONIC controller with the same reference-delivery settings. The reported motion-amplitude and velocity-variation measurements show why tracking should be examined beyond joint-position error. Because these are fixed-reference replays, their comparison addresses tracking of the supplied motion, not online music inference during execution. They are also separate from the current 38-dimensional ForeDance cohort. A matched recording of the current generated reference and its execution remains to be evaluated; the rendered sequence in Fig.~\ref{fig:sequence} is reference motion.
