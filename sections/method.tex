\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\method{} links musical forecasting, structured motion generation, and
continuation from committed motion. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the
common forecast condition and the motion context shared by these operations.

\paragraph{Streaming task}
At a planning decision, $a_{\leq\tau}$ is the arrived audio, $h$ is committed
motion-code history, and $b$ is the pose and velocity state derived from the
last committed reference frames. A frozen music model produces future states
$m^{\rm pred}=F(a_{\leq\tau})$. The generator samples
\begin{equation}
(q_{1:H},r_{1:H})\sim p_\theta(q,r\mid h,b,m^{\rm pred}),
\label{eq:conditional}
\end{equation}
where $q$ and $r$ are discrete structure and continuous detail. A shared decoder
maps them to native G1 motion. We commit the first $C<H$ latent steps, discard
the remaining plan, and update history and the reference state. Thus later decisions
may revise uncommitted plans while preserving the emitted motion.

\subsection{Anticipatory Music Conditioning}
\label{sec:anticipation}
The next movement will accompany sound that is only partly observed. We
provide prospective context by tokenizing arrived audio with SpectroStream
and rolling frozen MRT-2 Small forward~\cite{magenta2026mrt2}. Its general music
pretraining supplies the continuation prior; paired dance training learns how
to translate the predicted states into movement. The forecast covers
approximately 0.56\,s, alongside a motion plan of approximately 0.53\,s.
No future waveform samples enter the condition.

Each forecast state carries its intended time. Learned projections and time
embeddings align these states to motion positions, and frame-wise feature
modulation~\cite{perez2018film} conditions both branches:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FiLM}(v_i,c_i)=\gamma(c_i)\odot v_i+\beta(c_i).
\end{equation}
Here $v_i$ is a generator activation, $c_i$ its aligned music condition, and
$\gamma,\beta$ predict a scale and shift. Conditioning both branches lets the
same prospective context guide body organization and local articulation;
structure also conditions the detail generator. The music model stays frozen
while these motion-side mappings are learned. Expired forecast states are
masked by their timestamps. Appendix~\ref{app:training} specifies timing and
conditioning details.

\subsection{Structure--Detail Representation and Paired Training}
\label{sec:dc}
Musically directed motion needs coordinated body organization together with
local articulation. We learn these roles through complementary supervision,
using a discrete--continuous latent
\begin{equation}
z_i=e(q_i)+r_i,
\label{eq:latent}
\end{equation}
where $e(q_i)$ is a codebook embedding and $r_i$ a continuous residual. A shared
decoder $D_\psi(z,b)$ maps their sum and starting state to robot-native motion.
Quantization supplies a structural hypothesis; the residual adjusts movement
within it. The training objectives determine which properties this division
should preserve (Fig.~\ref{fig:dc}).

\paragraph{Learning complementary roles}
We pair full-latent reconstruction $D_\psi(e+r,b)$ with structure-only
reconstruction $D_\psi(e,b)$. The full path reconstructs all motion views,
whereas the structure-only path is supervised on designated structural views.
Both use the same decoder and starting state. This gives discrete structure
an explicit kinematic responsibility while allowing the combined latent to
retain complete movement.

The supervision views cover native coordinates and velocities, plus
forward-kinematics positions and velocities. In joint space, structure selects
root, legs, and waist; detail contains the arm coordinates. In geometry,
structure selects the torso, both wrists, and both sole points; detail contains
the remaining body points. Thus wrist placement can be structural while
arm-joint articulation belongs to the complementary native view. These are
supervision views of shared decoding, not separate anatomical decoders.
An auxiliary structural loss under residual corruption reinforces the same
organization when detail varies. Exact partitions, reconstruction gradients,
and loss weights are given in Appendix~\ref{app:training}.

\paragraph{Coupled motion generation}
A causal Transformer samples structural tokens. A residual diffusion
generator~\cite{ho2020ddpm} conditions on that sampled structure, the common
history and starting state, and the forecast music. Their sum decodes into
38-dimensional G1 motion, including root movement and 29 joint angles.
Both branches commit the same time segment, preserving their pairing across plans.
The next segment starts from a reference-derived pose and velocity state;
the coordinate convention and complete state definition are in
Appendix~\ref{app:training}.

\subsection{Commit Forcing}
\label{sec:cof}
Every committed segment changes both latent history and the reference state
from which the next segment begins. Teacher Forcing (TF) supplies these inputs
from recorded motion; streaming inference obtains them from generated motion.
\cof{} constructs this generated transition before supervising continuation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cof}). A stop-gradient first pass samples structure $\tilde q$
and detail $\tilde r$ with the inference samplers. Their jointly committed
segment is decoded, and an update operator $U$ constructs
\begin{equation}
(\tilde h^+,\tilde b^+)=U(h,b,\tilde q_{1:C},\tilde r_{1:C}).
\end{equation}
The next training pass receives the appended codes together with the state
induced by their movement. This keeps history and state consistent with the
same generated commitment rather than combining unrelated transitions.

\paragraph{Learning the next plan}
The next recorded latent remains the training target. For target
$z^*=e(q^*)+r^*$ and sampled next structure $\hat q$, we rebase the residual:
\begin{equation}
r^*_{\rm rebased}=z^*-e(\hat q).
\label{eq:rebase}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $e(\hat q)+r^*_{\rm rebased}=z^*$: rebasing preserves the target
latent. Decoded movement also depends on the starting state and need not be
identical under recorded and generated states. Each example independently
constructs its first-pass transition; the procedure trains continuation after
a generated commit without requiring a fully recursive training rollout.
The distinguishing operation is the joint history--state update, which exposes
the learner to the coupled consequences of its structure and detail predictions.

\paragraph{Supporting history regularization}
Full-history corruption (FHC) perturbs discrete embeddings and residuals across
the motion history, after CoF context construction. It leaves the reference
state and music condition unchanged and is disabled at inference.
Appendix~\ref{app:training} gives its settings.
